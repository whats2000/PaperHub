GISC: Sparse Instance Segmentation… TR-0412 Defect…

3 Method note

We segment each image into 64 × 64 pixel tiles and learn an embedding per tile. The prior assumes that defects are sparse within each tile, so at most a few pixels of a tile belong to an instance. This assumption keeps the contrastive loss well conditioned.

A contrastive prior pulls embeddings of the same instance together and pushes tiles without defects apart, which preserves recall on objects under 16 px where the detection head alone fails.

3.2 Training

We train for 40 epochs with AdamW at a learning rate of 1e-4 and a batch of 16 wafers. The contrastive weight λ is annealed from 0.1 to 0.5 over the first ten epochs, which avoids collapse early in training.

Figure 3 · recall vs. object size

Figure 3: Recall by object size. The prior helps most below 16 px.

Training takes 7% longer than the baseline; inference cost is unchanged since the prior is only used in the loss.

4. Inspection setup

The line inspects 300 mm wafers under dark-field illumination at 0.8 µm per pixel. Defects smaller than 10 µm are counted by a fixed-threshold detector tuned per lot.

We observed that scratch defects frequently cross tile boundaries and are split into several detections, inflating the count by up to 18% on lots with handling damage.

Recall below 10 µm drops sharply when the background texture changes between lots, which motivates a learned prior over defect shape.

Section 5 compares the threshold detector with two learned baselines on 1,240 annotated wafer maps.
